My thoughts on various writing utensils. This page is a WIP, and will presumably remain that way indefinitely. Let me know if you have any pen/pencil recommendations!

Gel Pens

Pentel Energel:

This is my go-to gel pen now. There are a number of variants, both capped and retractable. Be wary that some of the capped versions are non-refillable, but refillable ones do exist. I use 0.7 tip size, with either black or navy blue, and either capped (refillable) or retractable.

Pentel Sterling:

This is a classy-looking metal pen which takes Energel refills. It is capped, with both a black and silver model (of which I have the silver). The weight and look of the pen is very attractive. My main gripe so far is the narrow grip which doesn't feel super ergonomic.

Sharpie S-Gel:

This is the gel pen that first got me into the world of nice pens. Looking back, its not the greatest pen ever. The gel cartridges skip quite a bit. The pens look attractive though, so if you like the style, you could swap in an Energel refill!


Rollerball Pens

Pilot V5 Hi Tecpoint:

If you like a narrower pen with an inky flow, the V5 Hi Tecpoint is the way to go! They are not cartridge refillable, although I've seen people online refill them with a syringe. I haven't used many myself, but the one I do remember using started leaking before running out of ink, so the durability (for an edc pen) is potentially an issue.

Sharpie Roller:

Sharpie Rollers generally have a finder tip, which leads to a scratchier writing experience. Last time I tried one, I was of the mind where the less feedback, the better the writing experience, so these weren't my thing. I'd be interested in giving them another go. The body is once again attractive, perhaps even more so than Sharpie's S-Gel.

Uniball Vision Elite:

These pens are a joy to write with and have an attractive body. However, they relatively pricey, and the refills consist of an unfortunately substantial chunk of plastic.


Ballpoint Pens

Papermate Write Bros:

Generally I'm not a fan of the cheaper ballpoint pens and their oil-based ink. Papermate Write Bros is one that I've used a bit more, partially because of their heavier ink flow, and the lightweight nature of the pen body (which makes them feel a little less bad to throw away).

Fountain Pens

Lamy Safari:

A popular beginner fountain pen, this plastic, Bau-haus pen packs a punch. I have one Safari, with both a Medium and Extra Fine nib. The extra-fine nib is preferrable, especially for writing in smaller notebooks. The pen feels very portable, as it is both lightweight and durable.

Sheaffer Imperial (?):

An older pen that belonged to a late family member. It has a very unique design, with the nib integrated into the body of the pen. This Sheaffer puts down quite a fine line. The ink cartridges I recieved with it seem to take a long time to dry on the page. I may look into converting it for use with bottled inks down the road.

TWSBI Eco:

My first fountain pen, the Eco is a beauty. It is a demonstrator, allowing a view in to the inner workings of the pen. It holds a lot of ink, and has a beautifully made nib, mine in a Fine weight. I've stopped carrying it around since I acquired the Lamy Safari, but it remains a favourite for writing at my desk.

Zebra Disposable Fountain Pen

These pens are a cheap, disposable way to get one's toes wet in the world of fountain pens. While they are a bit scratchier on the page, I found them an enjoyable gateway pen.

Mechanical Pencils

Pentel Graphgear 1000:

This is the highest-end pencil I've owned so far. The metal design and retractable pipe make for a premium feeling, portable workhorse. Unfortunately, my pencil broke in the middle of the barrel, where a plastic piece connects two metal pieces (an unfortunate design flaw). I hope to either 3d print that piece or get it machined out of metal to restore my Graphgear.

Pentel Quick Click:

A decent pencil from Pentel. The plastic is quite cheap, but the Quick Click I had served me for quite a few years before the rubber grip started to deteriorate. The pipe is metal, and unretractable, so take care not to impale yourself!

Pentel Twist-Erase Click:

This pencil feels like its made with materials one step up from the Quick Click, with a slightly different mechanism. he pipe is metal, and unretractable, so take care not to impale yourself!
